# AI Carb Load Tracker: A Simple User Guide

A free AI coach that helps you plan your marathon carb load and keep track of the carbohydrates you eat and drink. It works in ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini. Setup takes about 5 minutes; logging a meal or snack usually takes about a minute.

No programming experience is needed.

## What the tracker helps you do

- **Make a plan:** Get a daily carbohydrate target and a meal plan based on your race and preferences.
- **Log food and drinks:** Tell your coach what you ate or drank, using text or photos when useful.
- **See your progress:** Check how many carbohydrates you've logged and how many remain to reach your daily target.
- **Choose what to eat next:** Get a practical suggestion based on the time of day and your progress.

A **carbohydrate target** is the amount of carbohydrates your coach recommends aiming for each day. Carbohydrates are often shortened to “carbs.”

## Before you start

You'll need:

- An account with ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini.
- An internet connection.
- Your race date.
- Your body weight.
- A few minutes to set up your coach.

**Important:** Sign in to your chosen AI platform before you begin. You'll use the same conversation throughout the carb-loading period so the coach can refer to the food you've already logged.

Free accounts may work, but photo uploads can be limited. You can do most tracking by typing.

## Step 1: Set up your coach

Set up the coach before your carb-loading period begins:

- For a **2-day carb load**, start setup **3 days before race day**.
- For a **3-day carb load**, start setup **4 days before race day**.

This gives you time to prepare the plan before the first day of carb loading.

1. **Choose your AI platform.** Open ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini and sign in.
2. **Copy the coach's instructions.** Open [`coach_prompt.md`](coach_prompt.md). Click **Copy raw file** (the button with two overlapping squares near the top right of the file).
3. **Start a new conversation.** Return to your AI platform, open a new chat, paste the copied instructions into the message box, and press **Send**.

   A *prompt* is simply a set of instructions that tells the AI how you want it to help you.
4. **Answer the setup questions.** Your coach will ask about your race date, body weight, preferred 2-day or 3-day load, food dislikes, and whether you want to track fluids. Answer in the chat, or complete a form if one appears.

Your coach will then calculate a daily carbohydrate target and prepare a plan for each day. Review the plan and ask questions if anything is unclear.

## Step 2: Log your food and drinks

Use the **same conversation** throughout the load. If you start a new chat, the coach may not have your earlier entries or totals.

Whenever you eat or drink something, send a message with the day, time, and what you had. Include portion sizes when you can.

**Example message:**

> Day 1, 8 am: I ate 1 plain bagel with 2 tablespoons of honey.

For packaged foods and drinks, the carbohydrate number on the nutrition label is usually the best information to provide. For restaurant meals, you can describe the meal or send a photo of the plate or menu.

### Example: logging a snack

**You type:**

> Day 1, 10 am: I drank one bottle of sports drink. The label says 34 g of carbs per bottle.

**Your coach should:**

- Add the drink to your Day 1 log.
- Update the running carbohydrate total and show how it compares with your daily target.
- Suggest what to eat or drink next.

This is an example of the interaction, not a calculated nutrition result. Your total depends on all the food and drinks you've logged.

### What the coach's response means

- **Daily table:** The items you've logged, with the estimated carbohydrates for each.
- **Carbs / target:** Your logged carbohydrate total compared with your daily goal.
- **“Y to go”:** An estimate of how many more grams you need to reach that goal.
- **“Next up”:** A suggestion for your next meal, snack, or drink.

Read the response after each entry. Check the numbers, and tell the coach if something looks wrong or if you forgot an item.

## Step 3: Get the best estimates

- **Packaged foods:** Type the number beside “Total Carbohydrate” on the nutrition label and how many servings you had. For example: “This bottle has 34 g of carbs; I drank one bottle.”
- **Food with a label photo:** If you can upload the label, make sure the “Total Carbohydrate” number and serving size are readable.
- **Restaurant meals or food without a label:** A photo or description can help the coach estimate carbohydrates, but the result will not be exact. Portion sizes and a familiar object for scale—such as a 20 oz bottle—can help.
- **When choosing from a menu:** Send a menu photo before ordering, or type 2–3 dishes you're considering and ask which best fits the plan. The coach should recommend options but should not log the meal until you tell it what you actually ate.
- **If an entry is unclear:** Give the coach more detail about the food or portion size. It should ask a follow-up question when it needs more information.

## Step 4: Save your photo uploads

AI platforms may limit how many photos you can upload, especially on free accounts. Limits can change, so don't rely on a specific number of uploads being available.

To make your uploads go further:

- **Packaged food or drinks:** Type the nutrition label instead of sending a photo whenever possible.
- **Restaurant food and menus:** Save photos for meals or menus that are hard to describe.
- **If a photo won't upload:** Type what you ate and include the label's “Total Carbohydrate” number and servings, if available. If you don't know the number, tell the coach what information you have rather than guessing.

## Step 5: Check your progress each day

After each food or drink entry:

1. Check that the item appears in the table.
2. Check the carbohydrate estimate and daily total.
3. Make sure the total reflects everything you've logged so far.
4. Read the **“Next up”** suggestion.

AI can make mistakes, including arithmetic mistakes. If a number looks wrong, ask the coach to show its calculation or recalculate the total from the full list of entries.

## Troubleshooting

**The coach seems to have forgotten what I ate.**  
Make sure you're in the same conversation you used for setup and earlier entries. Starting a new chat may mean the old log is unavailable.

**The total looks wrong.**  
Ask the coach to show the calculation and rebuild the total from the full list of logged foods and drinks. Check that serving sizes and label values are correct.

**The coach estimated a packaged food without using its label.**  
Provide the “Total Carbohydrate” number from the label and the number of servings you consumed, then ask it to update the entry.

**My photo won't upload.**  
Type the food name, portion size, and any nutrition information you have. You can keep tracking without a photo.

**I don't know what to order.**  
Type two or three menu options you're considering and ask the coach to compare them. You don't need to upload a menu photo.

**I forgot to log something.**  
Tell the coach the day and time, what you ate or drank, and the portion size. Ask it to add the missed entry and recalculate that day's total.

## Helpful tips

- Nutrition-label numbers are generally more reliable than estimates from food photos.
- If you're tired of eating, carbohydrate-containing drinks such as juice or sports drinks may be easier to consume. Choose options that work for you and that you have practiced tolerating.
- Keep familiar snacks available, especially when traveling.
- Don't assume a restaurant portion contains as many carbohydrates as it looks like it should; estimates can be off.
- Use the same chat and include the day and time in each entry.

## How the carbohydrate targets work

The tracker uses general sports-nutrition guidance to suggest targets. The current guidance described by this project is approximately:

- **2-day load:** 10–12 grams of carbohydrates per kilogram of body weight per day for 36–48 hours.
- **3-day load:** About 8 grams of carbohydrates per kilogram of body weight per day.

These are general guidelines, not a guarantee that a particular target is right for you. Follow the plan your coach generates only if it makes sense for your circumstances, and consult a qualified sports dietitian or healthcare professional if you are unsure.

## Important health and safety information

This coach provides general information, not medical advice. AI estimates and recommendations can be wrong; check food labels and use your judgment.

If you have diabetes, kidney disease, a stomach or bowel condition, are pregnant, or have a history of disordered eating, talk with a doctor or sports dietitian before attempting a carb load.

Carb-loading targets vary by person and event. If you are unsure what is appropriate, consult a qualified sports dietitian or healthcare professional.

On race morning, eat only foods and gels you have practiced with during training. Do not try new foods or products on race day.
